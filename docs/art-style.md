# Art style guide

All art is generated by `tools/art/` (Godot `Image` API, committed PNGs). Tickets 0024, 0025 and 0026 must follow this guide.

## View and scale
- Top-down with a slight 3/4 front face: objects show their front wall plus the top (roof, canopy, lid). Ground tiles are pure top-down.
- Tile = 32x32 px. Icons are 16x16. Settlers are about 12x16.
- Buildings, trees and settlers are anchored at the bottom of their footprint and may be taller than it (roofs and canopies extend upward). They share one y-sorted layer.
- Nearest-neighbour filtering only (project default). Never scale sprites by non-integer factors.

## Light
- Light comes from the top-left. Highlights on the top and left edges, shade on the bottom and right.
- Soft shadow cast to the bottom-right: `drop_shadow(1, 1)` with `Palette.SHADOW`, drawn under the object.
- No time-of-day lighting. A constant per-season color grade is allowed.

## Shading and texture rules
- Never use a flat fill for anything larger than 4x4 px. Use a ramp: `shade_rect`, `shade_ellipse`, `shade_polygon`, `gradient_v`, or `noise_fill` with a 3-5 step ramp.
- Add texture noise to large surfaces (ground, roofs, walls): `noise_fill` with scale 2-6 px, then `speckle` a few lighter and darker pixels.
- Use ordered (Bayer) dithering for gradients, never smooth blends.
- Every object gets a 1 px outline: `outline_dark()` (outline is a darkened version of the neighbouring pixel, mixed with `Palette.INK`). Ground tiles get no outline.
- Max 4-5 tones per material (taken from one palette ramp). Put the brightest tone only on top-left edges, a single pixel is enough.
- Prefer hand-placed detail pixels (knots, cracks, glints) over randomness; when randomness is used it must come from a seeded RNG.

## Palette
Defined in `tools/art/palette.gd` as ramps, ordered dark to light. Use only these colors.

| Ramp | Use |
|---|---|
| `GRASS` (5) | spring/summer ground, bushes, sprouts |
| `LEAF_DEEP` (4) | summer tree canopy, conifers |
| `AUTUMN` (4) | autumn foliage, pumpkins/fire accents |
| `WOOD` (5) | timber, logs, fences, tool handles |
| `STRAW` (4) | thatch, wheat, hay |
| `STONE` (5) | rocks, quarry, walls, cobbles |
| `ROOF_RED` (4) | clay tile roofs (barn, house accents) |
| `SLATE` (4) | slate roofs (blacksmith), night-blue shadows |
| `WATER` (5) | water, ice (lightened with `SNOW`) |
| `SNOW` (4) | snow caps, winter ground |
| `SAND` (3) | shorelines |
| `DIRT` (4) | soil, paths, fields |
| `STEEL` (4) | metal, ore, tools |
| `BERRY`, `MEAT`, `CREAM`, `FIRE`, `SKIN`, `FISH`, `BREAD`, `CABBAGE` | good icons, props, settlers, glow |
| `INK`, `SHADOW`, `CLEAR` | outline, drop shadow, transparent |

Seasonal variants are made by remapping ramps, not by new colors: spring `GRASS[3..4]` light, summer `GRASS[2..3]` + `LEAF_DEEP`, autumn `AUTUMN`, winter `SNOW` + `STONE`.

## Readability rules for buildings
- Each building has a unique silhouette (roof shape and height), a unique roof color/material, and one telling prop (see ticket 0025 table).
- A building must be identifiable at default zoom without its label.
- Roofs are the dominant element: roof color is the main identity cue (thatch=house, red=barn, dark green=forester, slate=blacksmith, hide=hunter, raw wood=woodcutter).
- Doors and windows use `WOOD[0..1]` and a light warm window (`FIRE[3]`) for contrast.

## Readability rules for goods icons
- Silhouette first: each good has a different outline and a different dominant hue (logs brown stack, stone grey blocks, iron steel-blue bars, firewood round log ends, tools crossed hammer and axe, wheat gold sheaf, cabbage green sphere, berries purple cluster, mushrooms brown caps, venison red haunch with bone, fish blue-silver fish).
- 16x16 canvas, content within x/y 1..14 so the outline fits.

## Settlers
- 12x16 sprites, clothing color identifies the profession; head `SKIN`, hair from `WOOD`/`STONE` (elder = light grey).
